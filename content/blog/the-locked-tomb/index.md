i read gideon the ninth this week. this is something i'd been meaning to read for a while now, and i'm pleased to report that it did not disappoint. i'd like to share some thoughts.

it's hard to boil this book to a single central thesis, but if i had to, i'd say it's about trust and loyalty. it is also an excellent whodunit for most of it's duration. this is all packaged in a fascinating science fantasy setting that is a delight to slowly unravel. and i can't fail to mention the large cast of characters that all feel fully realized, like they're alive. i don't think i can say much more without indulging in spoilers, so be warned.

{{< details summary="here be spoilers" >}}
i will format my thoughts in the form of an internet friendly numbered list:

1. much less focus on the lesbians part than i expected, for something that had that in the blurb on the cover.
2. gideon's flavour of loud-mouthed teenager isn't something i thought i'd like, but by the end, i came to love how straight up heroic she is.
3. harrow's brooding and sassy persona, on the other hand, i did think i'd like, and i was right. seeing how much of what how she presents - or how little, i suppose - is genuine was interesting.
4. i love all the different varieties of cavalier-necro relationships we got to see. all completely different from the others, and yet, all just as deep. except maybe the third house.
5. jeanne and isaac were adorable. jeanne, especially. can't say i didn't see their tragic ends coming, and can't say i enjoyed that, but i am thankful for them regardless. they felt like children when they needed to, and you could almost forget they'd been trying to join the military for years and that was what was expected of them.
6. i liked magnus and abigail. it's nice to see something resembling normal people in a setting as strange as this, and especially when they're normal and nice.
7. i enjoyed the eighth house. i was never quite sure where they were coming from, or where they were trying to go, but i liked what we got.
8. not much to say about the second house. they felt like they would have been the heroes of many a story, but not this one. oh, and seeing the reports on the other houses' candidates and knowing for a fact that palamedes was right when he said that they targetted the sixth because they thought she was the weakest combatant there was beautiful.
9. and the stars of the show, camilla and sex pal. i like when characters feel hyper-competent, and i like it even more when they are kind and compassionate at the same time. the second vs sixth duel was amazing. palamedes being the first one to figure out nearly every puzzle we come across was amazing. it would have been easy for this pair who seemingly know more than the others and are seemingly stronger than the others to have been boring by virtue of these facts, but they weren't. they were amazing.

in the time between starting to draft this post and actually finishing it, i have finished the next book in this series, harrow the ninth. maybe i will write something about that as well, i probably will, but i'd first like to sit on my thoughts on the story so far a bit longer. these are books that deserve to be ruminated upon, and have made me want to reread them immediately after i finished them more than any other book in my memory. it would be remiss of me to not to evangelize the locked tomb after how much i've enjoyed myself in it. this is that. now off i go to read the next one in the series.
